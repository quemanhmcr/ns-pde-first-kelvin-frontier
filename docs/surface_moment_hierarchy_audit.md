# Reverse-age oriented material-surface moment hierarchy audit

The finite-shape Kelvin descent error is now known exactly.  This note opens the
next physical layer without estimating it: how the **actual oriented material
surface moments themselves** evolve under the reverse-age Navier--Stokes
velocity-difference flow.

The result is rigid and unfavorable to premature finite closure:

- affine velocity closes every moment order on itself;
- a spatial velocity jet of degree `p>=2` makes the rate of moment order `m` depend
  on order `m+p-1`;
- exact smooth Navier--Stokes polynomial heat shears realize those couplings;
- material-anchor centering is not generally preserved;
- in a different exact shear geometry, an entire hidden moment tower can instead be
  conserved exactly.

Thus the infinite hierarchy is not bookkeeping invented around Navier--Stokes.  It
is generated by the literal material current kinematics.

No norm estimate, restart theorem, continuation theorem, or regularity conclusion
is used.

---

## 1. Literal reverse-age surface kinematics

Fix a stochastic material anchor `X` and write a material spanning surface in
relative coordinates `r`.  On reverse age,

\[
 dX=-u(X,r_{\rm phys})d\sigma+\sqrt{2\nu}\,dW,
\]

while the relative surface has zero martingale part and obeys

\[
\boxed{
\dot r
=-\Delta u_X(r),
\qquad
\Delta u_X(r):=u(X+r)-u(X).
}
\]

Let

\[
a_R:=n_R\,dA
\]

be the oriented area element.  For a material surface moving with relative velocity
`v_R=-Delta u_X`, the exact oriented-area transport law is

\[
\dot a_R
=[(\nabla_r\cdot v_R)I-(\nabla_rv_R)^T]a_R.
\]

In incompressible Navier--Stokes,

\[
\nabla_r\cdot v_R=0,
\qquad
\nabla_rv_R=-A(X+r),
\quad A=\nabla u,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\dot a_R=A(X+r)^T a_R.
}
\]

This is the finite-surface pointwise law whose integral gave the actual reverse-area
connection `+A^T` in the preceding descent audit.

**Status: Exact material-surface identity.**

## 2. Exact oriented monomial-moment transport law

For a multi-exponent

\[
\alpha=(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3),
\qquad
r^\alpha=\prod_i r_i^{\alpha_i},
\]

define the vector-valued oriented surface moment

\[
\boxed{
M_\alpha
:=\int_{\Sigma_R}r^\alpha a_R.
}
\]

Its physical order is

\[
|\alpha|=\sum_i\alpha_i.
\]

Differentiate `r^alpha a_R` using the two exact kinematic laws from Section 1:

\[
\boxed{
\frac d{d\sigma}(r^\alpha a_R)
=
-r^\alpha
\sum_i\alpha_i\frac{\Delta u_i(r)}{r_i}\,a_R
+r^\alpha A(X+r)^Ta_R,
}
\]

where the quotient notation means the ordinary monomial derivative and is better
written componentwise as

\[
\boxed{
\frac d{d\sigma}(r^\alpha a_R)
=
-\sum_i\alpha_i r^{\alpha-e_i}\Delta u_i(r)a_R
+r^\alpha A(X+r)^Ta_R.
}
\]

Integrating over the material surface gives the exact moment hierarchy:

\[
\boxed{
\dot M_\alpha
=
-\sum_i\alpha_i
\int r^{\alpha-e_i}\Delta u_i(r)a_R
+
\int r^\alpha A(X+r)^Ta_R.
}
\]

Every term has an immediate physical type:

- first sum: relative-position transport by the actual velocity difference;
- second integral: oriented-area transport by the spatially sampled velocity
  gradient.

There is no martingale shape source because the common Wiener translation lives
only in the anchor.

**Status: Exact identity.**

## 3. Affine velocity is the exact order-preserving case

Suppose locally and literally

\[
\Delta u_X(r)=Ar,
\qquad
A(X+r)=A
\]

with constant incompressible matrix `A`.  Then

\[
\boxed{
\dot M_\alpha
=A^TM_\alpha
-
\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_j A_{ij}
M_{\alpha-e_i+e_j}.
}
\]

Every moment on the right has exactly the same order:

\[
|\alpha-e_i+e_j|=|\alpha|.
\]

So affine flow does not need a closure ansatz: each full order-`m` oriented moment
tensor is an invariant finite-dimensional representation of the affine connection.
In particular, if every first oriented moment is zero, affine evolution preserves
that zero first-moment sector.

This includes the spatially uniform-gradient affine Navier--Stokes calibrations
already present in the repository.

**Status: Exact identity / exact affine closure theorem.**

## 4. Nonlinear velocity jets force upward moment coupling

Now take one homogeneous spatial velocity-difference jet of degree `p`:

\[
\Delta u^{(p)}(r)\sim r^p,
\qquad
\nabla\Delta u^{(p)}\sim r^{p-1}.
\]

For a moment of order `m=|alpha|`, both pieces in the exact rate law have degree

\[
\boxed{m+p-1.}
\]

Indeed,

\[
r^{m-1}\Delta u^{(p)}\sim r^{m+p-1},
\]

and

\[
r^m\nabla\Delta u^{(p)}\sim r^{m+p-1}.
\]

Hence a Taylor jet of order `p` contributes schematically

\[
\boxed{
\dot M^{(m)}
\supset
\mathcal C^{(p)}[M^{(m+p-1)}].
}
\]

The affine case `p=1` is exactly the order-preserving exception.  For `p>=2`, a
lower moment requires a higher moment.

For a smooth velocity with many nonzero spatial jets, the exact hierarchy therefore
has the form

\[
\boxed{
\dot M^{(m)}
=
\mathcal C^{(1)}M^{(m)}
+
\mathcal C^{(2)}M^{(m+1)}
+
\mathcal C^{(3)}M^{(m+2)}+\cdots
}
\]

with Taylor remainder when the expansion is finite-order rather than analytic.
This is an **upward-coupled hierarchy**: low-order geometry is not dynamically closed
under nonlinear spatial variation.

CI referees the degree statement for the divergence-free homogeneous shear family

\[
\Delta u=(y^p,0,0),
\qquad p=2,\ldots,6,
\]

and moment orders `m=0,...,4`.  The nonzero rate is exactly proportional to
`y^(m+p-1)`.

**Status: Exact homogeneous-polynomial identity; rigorous local jet consequence for
smooth Navier--Stokes.**

This is a structure theorem, not a singular-time estimate.

## 5. Exact quadratic heat-shear NS destroys material-anchor centering

Consider the exact smooth heat shear

\[
\boxed{u=(y^2+2\nu t,0,0).}
\]

Its advection vanishes and `U_t-nu U_yy=0`, so it is an exact Navier--Stokes
solution with constant pressure.

At anchor `y=0`, the relative velocity difference is

\[
\Delta u=(y^2,0,0),
\]

and

\[
A^Te_x=2y\,e_y.
\]

Take a centered `yz` rectangle with initial normal `e_x` and half-widths `b,c`.
Its oriented first `y`-moment is initially

\[
M_y(0)=\int y\,e_x\,dA=0.
\]

But the exact hierarchy gives

\[
\boxed{
\dot M_y(0)
=
2\int y^2\,dA\,e_y
=
rac{8}{3}b^3c\,e_y
\neq0.
}
\]

So **material-anchor centering is not dynamically preserved** by nonlinear
Navier--Stokes, even in a globally smooth exact solution.

The source is exactly the quadrupole, as the `p=2`, `m=1` order-raising rule predicts.

**Status: Audited calibration (exact Navier--Stokes) / rigorous centering no-go.**

## 6. A single anchor shift cannot generically recenter vector-valued oriented moments

Collect first moments into the matrix

\[
F_{ia}
:=\int r_i(n_R)_a\,dA,
\]

and write the oriented area vector as

\[
h_a=\int(n_R)_a\,dA.
\]

Under a single anchor shift

\[
r'=r-c,
\]

one has exactly

\[
\boxed{
F'=F-c h^T.
}
\]

Therefore all oriented first moments can be centered by one anchor shift iff

\[
\boxed{F=c h^T}
\]

for some vector `c`.

That is a rank-one compatibility condition.  A generic vector-valued oriented first
moment matrix does not satisfy it.

The quadratic heat-shear calibration from Section 5 shows the physical mechanism:
initially `h` points along `e_x`, while the newly generated `M_y` points along
`e_y`.  An anchor shift subtracts only a multiple of `h` from that row, so it cannot
remove the new transverse component at first order.

Thus “recenter the material surface” is itself an observer operation with
nontrivial admissibility conditions; it is not a free gauge transformation on the
full oriented-current state.

**Status: Exact geometry / audited exact-NS obstruction.**

## 7. The opposite exact shear geometry conserves an entire hidden moment tower

Now use any shear

\[
u=(U(y,t),0,0)
\]

and an `xy` spanning surface with normal `e_z`.  The reverse relative velocity is
purely in `x`, so every relative `y` coordinate is constant.  Moreover

\[
A^Te_z=0.
\]

Hence for **every** integer `m>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac d{d\sigma}
\int_{\Sigma_R} y^m e_z\,dA=0.
}
\]

CI checks the exact pointwise rate for `m=0,...,7` without choosing a particular
`U`.

This explains the cubic heat-shear covariance-blind calibration from the previous
note.  Its Kelvin descent bias is the contraction of the conserved `m=2` moment with
`partial_yy omega`; in that exact solution the contraction itself becomes the
constant

\[
\varepsilon_K=-4ab^3.
\]

So nonlinear NS can do either of two literal things depending on geometry:

- **generate higher shape moments into lower-order observables**;
- **carry a hidden higher moment as an exactly conserved finite-shape mode**.

Neither behavior is visible from covariance alone.

**Status: Exact shear identity / physical mechanism calibration.**

## 8. Polynomial Kelvin bias, shape drift, and noise are exact jet--moment contractions

For a polynomial local vorticity profile

\[
\omega(X+r)
=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha r^\alpha,
\]

the finite-shape Kelvin descent error is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_K
=
\sum_{|\alpha|\ge1}
c_\alpha\cdot M_\alpha.
}
\]

No approximation is involved for polynomial fields.

Likewise, expanding

\[
A(X+r)-A(X)
=\sum_{|\alpha|\ge1}A_\alpha r^\alpha
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_A
=\sum_{|\alpha|\ge1}A_\alpha^TM_\alpha.
}
\]

And the stochastic error coefficient is the same Kelvin contraction after one
spatial derivative:

\[
\boxed{
q_\mu^{\rm err}
=
\sum_{|\alpha|\ge1}
(\partial_\mu c)_\alpha\cdot M_\alpha.
}
\]

For smooth non-polynomial fields these formulas become finite Taylor contractions
plus the literal integral remainder; the exact surface integral remains primary.

The cubic heat-shear tests recover simultaneously:

- the Kelvin bias from the oriented `M_yy` quadrupole;
- the reverse shape residual from that same quadrupole on the orthogonal `yz`
  surface;
- zero error-noise coefficient on the centered `xy` surface because the required
  first moment is a **known zero state component**, not an omitted component.

This last point is important for any reduced model: “not stored” and **physically zero** are different statements.

**Status: Exact polynomial identity / exact physical typing.**

## 9. Why no finite low-moment state is dynamically invariant in generic nonlinear flow

Suppose one stores all oriented moments only through order `N`.  If the velocity has
a nonzero quadratic spatial jet, the exact equation for order `N` already contains
order `N+1`.  If a cubic jet is active, it contains order `N+2`; and so on.

Thus no cutoff

\[
\{M^{(0)},M^{(1)},\ldots,M^{(N)}\}
\]

is generically invariant under the literal nonlinear surface generator.

This dynamic result is stronger than the earlier static Legendre counterexample:

- the Legendre family says two finite surfaces can agree in all stored moments but
  have different exact NS response;
- the moment transport theorem says the omitted moments are also *called directly
  by the time derivative* of the stored moments whenever the corresponding velocity
  jet is active.

Exact polynomial heat shears realize arbitrarily high spatial degree while remaining
smooth exact Navier--Stokes solutions.  Therefore this is not a pathology imported
from an artificial velocity field.

**Status: Rigorous consequence of the exact hierarchy plus audited exact-NS
polynomial family.**

It does not say that a shrinking support cannot make the hierarchy asymptotically
local.  That is the remaining theorem.

## 10. Ledger placement and next structural target

The hierarchy now separates four questions that must not be conflated.

### Full finite shape

The embedding/current `R(.)` is exact state.  Its oriented moment tower is a family
of observables of that state.

**Status: Exact physical state.**

### Finite moment truncation

A finite collection of low moments is not a universal exact quotient state for
nonlinear NS surface transport.

**Status: Rigorous no-go consequence / exact NS calibration family.**

### Centered moment expansion

Centering can simplify a fixed-state Taylor contraction, but material centering is
not automatically preserved, and a single anchor shift cannot generically center a
vector-valued oriented first-moment matrix.

**Status: Exact geometry / audited calibration.**

### First-bad shrinking-support descent

It remains possible that the *actual selected material support* shrinks and stays
conditioned strongly enough that the entire omitted tower becomes asymptotically
subordinate to the local jet.  No such uniform theorem has been proved.

The next structural question is therefore not “which finite moment should we add?”
but:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does the literal first-bad material support enforce a scale/shape law that}
\quad
M^{(m)} / \ell^{m+2}
\quad
\text{remains uniformly controlled across all }m?
}
\]

or, alternatively, does exact Navier--Stokes furnish a material-support history that
violates any such jet-collapse mechanism?

That question should be attacked first through exact support/moment transport and
adversarial NS calibrations, before any norm estimate.

**Status: Open-literal.  No restart/continuation/regularity theorem claimed.**

## 11. Full codeforming reduction of the infinite tower

The raw upward-coupled hierarchy admits an exact stronger reorganization.  Let the
local reverse line frame satisfy `L_dot=-A(X)L`, set `xi=L^-1 r` and pull oriented
area back by `cof(L)^-1`.  Then every affine connection term cancels and the whole
pulled-back tower is transported by the single divergence-free nonaffinity field

\[
\mathcal N_L(\xi)=L^{-1}[u(X+L\xi)-u(X)-A(X)L\xi].
\]

The pulled-back area law is exactly `(D_xi mathcal N_L)^T`; an exponential generating
current packages every oriented moment.  Coherent linear refinement leaves the full
pulled-back tower invariant.  Homogeneous degree-`p` jets enter as
`rho^(p-1) S^-1 U_p(S xi)` for `L=rho S`, exposing scalar scale and anisotropy as
separate physical faces.

Exact linear-strain and quadratic heat-shear NS calibrations then show two different
no-go facts: codeforming constancy does not imply support locality, while support
locality alone does not force codeforming affine collapse under severe anisotropy.

**Status: Exact identity / audited exact-NS calibrations.  First-bad anisotropic
jet-frame collapse remains Open-literal.  No restart/continuation/regularity theorem
claimed.**
